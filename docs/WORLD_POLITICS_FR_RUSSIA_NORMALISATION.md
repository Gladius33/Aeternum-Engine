# France–Russia normalisation: diplomatic, legal and economic design

Reference date 2026-11-25. France is the only playable state in the
initial release. Russia, Ukraine, other EU members, third countries,
EU institutions and other relevant organisations are autonomous game
actors. This is a counterfactual game design, not a real-world political
recommendation or a forecast of any government's decisions. Actual law
and dated observations are separated from alternative scenario events.

## Baseline and scope

2021 is a pre-full-scale-invasion comparison year, not a sanction-free
baseline: several restrictions already existed before February 2022.
The French Treasury's 1 July 2026 note reports French goods imports from
Russia at EUR 4.1bn and exports at EUR 1.89bn in 2025, down respectively
59.6% and 70.9% from 2021. Approximate reconstructions from the rounded
data are 10.15bn and 6.49bn, respectively, not primary 2021 observations.
The SDES reports Russia at 22% of French natural gas imports in 2021
and 17% in 2025, crude oil at 9% in 2021 and zero since 2023; coal
30% in 2021 and zero since 2023. Gas import volumes include re-exports,
and are not final French consumption. 2025 gross imports 518 TWh PCS,
exports 183 TWh PCS. Keep SDES physical and customs statistical
definitions separate; no direct mixing of HHV and LHV. Treasury reports
2025 natural gas/LNG EUR 3.17bn, nitrogen products EUR 431.3m and
non-ferrous metals EUR 163.7m in imports. Pharmaceuticals, perfumes,
cosmetics and some agricultural products are still exported. No
presumption of a complete embargo or of automatic contract revival.
Sector data and old contract terms are not interchangeable. Collect
dated public volumes, price benchmarks, contractual and infrastructure
status; confidential commercial terms stay explicitly unknown.

## Independent legal/diplomatic tracks

A French bilateral normalisation is not a Russia–Ukraine peace treaty.
France can offer talks, mediation, diplomatic contacts and agreements
within its competence. Ukraine and Russia must independently consent
to any settlement between them. Security guarantees and verification
are separate terms, not forced upon another actor. The documented
10 Nov 2026 European/Ukraine/US coordination emphasises sovereignty,
territorial integrity and security guarantees; actual future outcomes
remain hypothetical. Under French Constitution articles 52–55,
negotiation/ratification and legislative authorisation for treaty classes
are distinct. Cohabitation and cabinet authority matter.

Represent restrictions by jurisdiction, instrument, subject, goods,
service, counterparties, effective dates, exemptions, and status.
EU CFSP decisions generally use unanimity; implementing regulations
under article 215 TFEU use the applicable treaty procedure (qualified
majority). Do not confuse non-renewal, repeal, suspension, exemption
and non-enforcement. Separate each instrument, including 2014 measures,
individual listings and asset freezes, sector restrictions, export controls,
financial services, transport and anti-circumvention rules. Other
jurisdictions' measures cannot be rescinded by France. EU common
commercial policy is an exclusive EU competence. Russian countermeasures
are distinct. Repeal requires the appropriate legal act and effective date.

Regulation (EU) 2026/261 is an independent legislative constraint on
Russian gas imports. Council timeline: new contracts banned from
18 March 2026; transitional provisions for eligible existing contracts;
long-term LNG ban 1 January 2027; pipeline long-term ban 30 September
2027 (conditional 1 November 2027 extension). Treat exact eligibility
and exemptions as legal-registry fields, not player assertions.
A sanctions repeal or diplomatic agreement alone does not repeal this
regulation. For complete restoration of ordinary lawful trade, all
applicable legal gates must be cleared, including export controls and
sector-specific rules. No disguised origin, prohibited intermediary,
evasion, illicit payment or cargo-routing mechanic. A blocked legal
gate stays blocked until a competent authority changes it or issues
a valid permission.

## Player journeys (alternative branches, not recommendations)

A. Diplomatic normalisation within the EU, commercial status quo:
reopen talks, negotiate bilateral non-restricted cooperation, maintain
other instruments, and allow the other actors to accept/reject. May
result in diplomatic progress without energy trade changes.

B. Collective phased restoration inside the EU: France proposes an
EU review with independently negotiated peace/security terms where
relevant. Council and other competent institutions handle each instrument
and the gas phase-out legislation separately. Ukraine and Russia negotiate
their own settlement. Other states may object, abstain, agree, propose
amendments or decline to provide guarantees. Conditional partial repeal
can be modelled by product classes and explicit effective dates.
New lawful contracts and capacity allocations follow only after
clearance. Full restoration is one possible outcome, not a scripted one.

C. Withdrawal of France from EU (and optionally euro): uses the prior
WP exit study's Article 50 legal clock and monetary transition.
Before effective withdrawal, EU law continues to apply. After withdrawal,
France can revise its own restrictions under domestic and international
law; EU territory, counterparties, services and transit remain subject to
their applicable law. No automatic access to foreign markets, grids,
banks, ships, insurance or customs privileges. Independent agreements,
jurisdictional checks, trade and finance settlement and actual available
infrastructure are still required. Exit does not require a simultaneous
monetary switch, and does not itself settle the Ukraine war.

D. Failure/partial outcome: diplomatic talks but no agreement, veto,
expired mandate, continuing EU legislation, third-country refusal,
commercial arbitration, unavailable infrastructure, insufficient capacity,
financial or insurance refusal, domestic legislative rejection or a new
crisis. Offer ordinary lawful re-negotiation, agreed modifications,
defer/reassess, or diversified legal supply; preserve all restrictions
where not actually lifted. Refusal is meaningful, not an automatically
solvable obstacle. Include suspension/termination and snapback only
when provided for by actual simulated legal acts.

E. Rapid versus staggered lawful commercial recovery: after legal
clearance, compare 1/3/6/12/24/36 month operational ramps and independent
negotiation durations. Legacy target at 25/50/75/100% of independently
verified historical physical volumes, conditional on supplier acceptance,
domestic demand and available capacity. Include 0%, supply reduction and
new terms; no automatic pre-2022 price or guaranteed volume. No simulated
trade before permissions, physical readiness and contractual effect.

## Physical supply network and trade model

Model public aggregate infrastructure categories with a dated status:
maritime LNG, regasification, gas transmission interconnection,
storage; maritime crude and refined oil, refinery compatibility,
domestic distribution; ordinary civilian goods road, rail, maritime
and air subject to legal permissions. Legal authorisation is a hard
constraint independent of physical topology. Historical east-west
pipeline routes do not establish present availability: IEA documents
Yamal-Europe interruption and Nord Stream damage in 2022; Ukraine
transit ceased 1 January 2025. Require independent rights, operator
agreements, verified availability and any necessary works before
restoring an unavailable link. France's operating LNG terminals and
gas interconnectors exist but capacity is not unreserved supply.
Commercial market zone and physical gas origin are not interchangeable.
Do not provide sanction circumvention paths; use abstract network edges
and lawful aggregate capacity data, not tactical carrier or routing advice.
Unknown capacity is unknown/closed for committed supply, not infinite.

For each simulation day and commodity, conservation:
opening_stock + production + authorised_received_imports
= served_domestic_demand + exports + losses + closing_stock.
Track unsatisfied demand separately, and storage capacity and injection/
withdrawal limits. Contract nomination <= agreed volume. Received
volume <= nominated, available supply, cleared capacity and recipient
demand/storage. Multi-commodity flow capacity shares must not be sold
twice. Energy units and currency conversions explicit. Separate
commercial and physical origin, gross and net flows, quantities,
transport costs, tariffs, losses, taxes and end-user price. No free
capacity invented by an improved diplomatic relationship.

Delivered cost = commodity purchase + legitimate logistics + conversion
or refining/regasification + insurance + applicable duties and fees.
Compare delivered costs and supply reliability against identical
counterfactual baseline (no normalisation). An illustrative 20 TWh
annual substitution at EUR 10/MWh lower delivered cost gives EUR 200m
gross annual purchase-cost difference, before other effects. Not an
observed saving or a price forecast. Sensitivity +/- EUR 5/10/20 per
MWh, 0/25/50/75/100% of independently verified volume target, world
price shocks, exchange-rate risk, substitution and demand elasticity.
No claim of an automatic pump-price or household-bill reduction.
Sector output, import costs, exports, fiscal revenue, household CPI,
real income, energy security, investment and concentration are outcomes
from the macro engine. New agreements may diversify suppliers or
increase concentration; existing alternative supplier contracts can
impose costs when changed.

## Autonomous negotiation and reaction engine

Represent institutions separately from member states. Each country/
organisation receives offers with version/hash, scope, legal competence,
effective date, commitments, reciprocal obligations and verification
terms. Actor profiles use public dated starting evidence, not assumed
ethnic/ideological personality or predicted actual behaviour. Maintain
state variables: relationship, treaty commitments, security concerns,
energy demand/available capacity, economic interests, internal legal
constraints, domestic political calendar, verification/trust and prior
compliance. Only permitted simulated state and public facts enter AI.

Each actor may accept, reject, counteroffer, defer, request verification,
invoke a legal procedure or withdraw. Hard legal competence and
applicable restrictions override soft preference scores. Neither Russia
nor Ukraine automatically accepts a French proposal. EU decision
rules are separate from bilateral negotiations. A transit government
can refuse a future agreement, and an independent operator may have
no capacity even with political agreement. US/UK and other non-EU
actors retain their own laws; changes in France–Russia relations do
not change their policies automatically. Existing NATO obligations
are not erased by trade decisions or EU withdrawal.

Use bounded deterministic preference scores and scenario seeds, logged
reason codes and counteroffer limits. Calibrate initial profiles only
with sourced policy statements; alternative future game decisions
derive from game state, not real-world predictions. No forced favourable
or punitive response based on a political label. Explain rejection:
legal instrument still active, consent absent, security terms rejected,
contract incompatible, capacity unavailable, financing not approved,
or domestic authorisation missing. Player sees legal/physical/commercial
states independently. AI uses same legal gates as player.

## Proposed acceptance cases

1. Bilateral diplomatic agreement leaves EU sanctions and gas
   regulation intact; prohibited deliveries remain zero.
2. CFSP non-renewal or repeal alone does not unlock a distinct gas ban;
   competent legislative change and effective date required.
3. A peace agreement without Ukrainian consent cannot settle the
   Russia–Ukraine conflict. A bilateral French agreement is independent.
4. EU withdrawal notification alone changes neither member-state
   obligations nor route eligibility; effective withdrawal changes only
   relevant French jurisdiction.
5. Third-country refusal or physically closed link remains a real
   blocker despite French/Russian agreement.
6. Legacy agreement expired/terminated: no automatic price or
   volume recovery. New contract and verified capacity required.
7. Partial or staged lifting affects only covered products, persons
   and effective days; separate authorities cannot be impersonated.
8. Same seed, same evidence, same history. No duplicated stock,
   financing, bookings, tax or macro-economic gains.
9. Trade remains blocked with unknown legal clearance or capacity.
   Mid-contract reimposition follows applicable legal/contract rules.
10. Compare maintained sanctions, partial restoration, full lawful
    restoration and failed talks over 1–120 months, with common shocks.
11. Foreign governments remain AI-controlled; no player takeover.
12. No trade/cash flow or legal status change before the appropriate
    authority, date, and verified event. Existing alpha scenarios intact.
